At the **OCETA 2025** conference, I delivered a talk focused on the phenomenon of ***embodied presence*** within Virtual and Extended Reality (XR) – examining the biological and perceptual mechanisms through which human bodily senses enter and inhabit digital environments.

---

## Core Topics Covered

### 1. Objective Immersion Telemetry via Biometric Sensors
In conventional game development, immersion is typically evaluated through retrospective subjective surveys. In my talk, I demonstrated methodologies for capturing real-time physiological signals utilizing certified biomedical instrumentation (Empatica E4) alongside custom ESP32 telemetry hardware:
* Capturing electrodermal activity (**EDA / GSR**) as an indicator of sympathetic nervous system arousal.
* Heart rate dynamics (**HR**) and micro-fluctuations in epidermal skin temperature.

### 2. Empirical Research Findings
I presented comparative trial results contrasting identical interactive setups across standard desktop monitors versus immersive VR head-mounted displays. The data revealed dramatic surges in EDA amplitude within VR, empirically demonstrating deep engagement of motor and emotional circuits.

### 3. Practical Mechanics for Spatial Depth in XR
The latter portion offered actionable design strategies for XR developers and engineers:
* **Tactile & Physics-Driven Interactions:** Replacing simple controller button presses with two-handed manipulation of physical levers, dials, and climbable surfaces.
* **Multisensory Expansion:** Designing sensory stimuli beyond sight and sound — including early telemetry insights from custom embedded olfactory displays.
* **Low-Latency Multiplayer Architecture:** Guidelines for multiplayer VR (Netcode for GameObjects) leveraging distributed client-side authority to eradicate motion sickness.
