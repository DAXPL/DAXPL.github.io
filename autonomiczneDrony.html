About the Project

The project aims to create an environment for designing unmanned vehicles powered by artificial intelligence. Training drones in a virtual environment before deploying them in the real world is an approach gaining popularity, especially thanks to new tools and breakthroughs in the fields of simulation and artificial intelligence. The massive computing power offered by modern hardware also enables simulations on an unprecedented scale, while maintaining high precision. For business, however, the most important argument for "dry" prototyping in a computer is price. Simulations allow for testing control algorithms without the risk of equipment damage, space rental costs, or danger to the surroundings.


As part of the project, we used the Unity ML-Agents Toolkit – a set of tools enabling the training of agents in 3D environments using AI techniques, specifically Reinforcement Learning (RL).


One of the key challenges and simultaneously the most ambitious goals of the XR BINIU project is the communication architecture. Initially, during prototyping and local testing, we relied on the built-in capabilities of the ESP32 microcontroller, which connected to the control application via Wi-Fi. Communication took place through the WebSocket protocol, which worked well during tests on campus, but this solution had two major flaws: connection instability and a fundamental range limitation to a single local network. We are currently migrating to a much more scalable architecture based on implementing a GSM/LTE modem on the watercraft. This frees the drone from the local Wi-Fi range and gives it access to the global cellular network via VPN.

Application of 3D Printing

3D printing technology played a fundamental role in the project, enabling rapid prototyping and the manufacturing of custom structural elements. We used FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) technology to create the drone's hull and numerous auxiliary components, such as motor mounts, superstructures, a turret, and aerial propulsion arms. The choice of this technology was dictated by its availability, low operating costs, and the ability to quickly implement design changes based on buoyancy and balance tests.

Special thanks go to the Wielkopolskie Centrum Nowych Technologii (Greater Poland Center for Advanced Technologies) for their invaluable help in realizing this project. Providing the team with an industrial Dragon 3D printer and material support in the form of filament allowed us to print the full-sized hull in one piece, which was crucial to the success of the entire endeavor. Your support and openness to student initiatives allowed us to turn an ambitious project into reality.

My Contribution and Responsibilities

In the XR BINIU project, my main task was programming the modular Sim2Real architecture. The key here was creating a universal "virtual brain" (Breadboard) that separates AI logic from physical hardware. My second role was the implementation and supervision of the Reinforcement Learning process. I used the Unity ML-Agents package for this.

Future of the Project

The launching of the water drone was a huge success and confirmation of the correctness of our Sim2Real architecture. Currently, together with the team, we are raising the bar and applying the same principles to a much more ambitious goal: teaching AI to fly a bicopter. This is an extremely difficult challenge, as a dual-rotor drone (assisted by servomechanisms) is inherently unstable. Once again, we are training the agent in a precise Unity simulation before deploying the model on the physical device.
